## Key Methodological Findings

- Pure self-attention networks (SANs) without skip connections or MLPs converge to rank-1 matrices doubly exponentially with depth, causing token uniformity where all output tokens become identical
- Skip connections are crucial in preventing rank collapse by providing short paths (length 0) that preserve residual information; this reveals a previously unknown vital effect beyond facilitating optimization
- MLPs counteract rank collapse by increasing their Lipschitz constant, creating a tug-of-war with self-attention layers, though this comes at the cost of reduced robustness to input perturbations
- Layer normalization does not mitigate rank collapse as it only rescales features and cannot increase matrix rank
- Transformers behave like ensembles of shallow networks: short paths (length 1-2) carry most predictive power (>80% accuracy on memorization task), while long paths (length >3) perform near random
- Theoretical convergence bound requires 4γβH < sqrt(d_qk), but experiments show rank collapse occurs more broadly in practice, suggesting the theoretical condition is pessimistic
- Architectural variants like low-rank attention approximations may converge even faster to rank-1, while sparse attention patterns may slow convergence due to increased rank
